Why a home loan is priced so differently

A home loan is secured against the property itself, and property is the collateral lenders like most — it does not move, it is registered, and it usually appreciates. That security is the entire reason the rate is roughly half of what an unsecured personal loan costs.

Most home loans today are floating-rate and linked to the RBI repo rate, currently 5.50%. Your rate is the repo rate plus a spread that the lender fixes based on your credit profile and loan size. When the repo moves, your EMI or your tenure moves with it. In November 2026 public sector banks are quoting from about 7.35%, large private banks and housing finance companies from about 7.60% to 7.75%.

Lenders will fund up to 90% of the property value for smaller tickets, 80% in the mid band and 75% above ₹75 lakh — the balance is your down payment, and it must come from your own resources. Then there is stamp duty and registration, typically 5% to 7% of the agreement value depending on the state, which no lender funds. Budget for both before you commit to a property.

Borrow for thirty years because you can, and you will pay roughly double the house in interest. Borrow for twenty and prepay once a year, and you will own it in fourteen.

What people use it for

  • Buying a ready-to-move or under-construction flat or house
  • Constructing a house on a plot you already own
  • Buying a residential plot, with or without a construction plan
  • Extending, renovating or repairing an existing home
  • Transferring an expensive existing home loan to a cheaper lender
  • Taking a top-up on an existing home loan at near home-loan rates

Types of Home Loans we arrange

The label matters, because each variant is underwritten and priced differently — and applying under the wrong one costs you both rate and time.

Home purchase loan

For a ready or under-construction property from a builder or a resale seller. The standard product, and the most competitively priced.

Construction loan

For building on a plot you own. Disbursed in stages against construction progress certified by the lender's engineer.

Plot & composite loan

For buying land, optionally bundled with construction finance. Plot-only loans carry a slightly higher rate and shorter tenure.

Balance transfer

Move an existing loan to a lender quoting lower. On a large outstanding, even 0.4% saved runs into lakhs over the remaining tenure.

Top-up loan

Additional funds on an existing home loan, at a rate far below a personal loan, for renovation, education or business.

Home improvement / extension

For repair, renovation, an extra room or a floor. Priced close to a standard home loan and eligible for tax relief on interest.

Who qualifies

Home loan eligibility is driven by two separate tests — your repayment capacity, and the property's legal and technical soundness. A perfect borrower with a defective title still gets declined.

Applicant criteria

  • Age 21 to 70 years at loan maturity
  • Salaried: 2+ years of work, 1 year in the current job
  • Self-employed: 3+ years of business with filed ITRs
  • CIBIL 750+ for the best pricing; 700+ generally workable
  • Total EMIs within 50–60% of net monthly income
  • Resident Indian or NRI (NRI terms differ)

Property criteria

  • Clear and marketable title, with the full chain of ownership
  • Approved building plan and occupancy certificate where applicable
  • Property falls within the lender's approved geography
  • Builder or project approved by the lender, for under-construction units
  • Satisfactory technical valuation and legal opinion
  • No encroachment, litigation or unapproved construction

  • Latest property tax receipt and encumbrance certificate
  • NOC from the builder or society
  • Own-contribution proof — receipts and bank debits

How to get a better deal

Chase the spread, not the headline rate

Every floating home loan is repo plus a spread. The repo is the same for everyone; the spread is what you are actually negotiating. Ask each lender to state the spread in writing, then compare those numbers.

One extra EMI a year is worth years

Paying thirteen EMIs instead of twelve, every year, on a twenty-year loan takes roughly four to five years off the tenure. There is no prepayment charge on a floating individual loan, so this costs you nothing but discipline.

Put the higher earner first, and consider a woman co-applicant

The primary applicant's profile drives the rate. Many lenders also offer a 5 basis-point concession where a woman is the primary applicant or co-owner — small, but it runs for thirty years.

Review the loan every two years

Spreads tighten over time for new customers while existing borrowers sit on old ones. A conversion fee of 0.25% to move down half a percent pays for itself within months. Send us your statement and we will do the arithmetic.

Home Loans — your questions answered

A practical rule is 50 to 60 times your net monthly income, adjusted for your existing EMIs and your age. On ₹80,000 a month with no other loans, eligibility typically lands between ₹45 lakh and ₹55 lakh over twenty years. Clubbing a working spouse's income raises this substantially, and a longer tenure raises it further — though at a real cost in total interest. Send us your income details and obligations and we will give you a worked figure rather than a rule of thumb.

For a long-tenure home loan, floating is the mainstream choice. It is cheaper at the start, it moves down when the repo rate falls, and — importantly — it carries no prepayment charge for individual borrowers. Fixed-rate loans buy certainty at a premium of one to two percentage points and usually lock you in for a few years. A small number of lenders offer hybrid products, fixed for an initial period and floating after.

A balance transfer moves your outstanding loan to a lender offering a lower rate. It is worth doing when the saving is at least 40 to 50 basis points and you still have a good number of years left, because most of the interest in a home loan is paid in the early years. Factor in the new lender's processing fee, fresh legal and technical charges, and the paperwork. On a ₹50 lakh outstanding with fifteen years left, half a percent saved is roughly ₹3 lakh. Send us your sanction letter and statement and we will do the comparison honestly, including when it is not worth it.

The loan is calculated on the agreement value or the valuation, whichever is lower, and stamp duty plus registration — five to seven per cent depending on the state — is your out-of-pocket cost. Some lenders will include it for affordable-segment loans, and a few will factor registration into the property cost for smaller tickets. Plan for it as a cash requirement rather than hoping the lender absorbs it.

Yes, though the route differs. Banks underwrite strictly off filed income, so a modest ITR produces a modest sanction. Housing finance companies and affordable-housing lenders assess banking turnover, business activity and cash flow alongside the ITR, and can often sanction meaningfully more. The rate is somewhat higher. This is precisely the kind of case where going to the right lender first — rather than collecting rejections from banks — changes the outcome.

Up to thirty years on paper, but the real limit is your age at maturity — usually sixty for salaried applicants and sixty-five to seventy for self-employed. A thirty-five-year-old salaried borrower can realistically get twenty-five years; a fifty-year-old gets ten. Adding a younger earning co-applicant can extend the tenure, which is a common and legitimate way to improve both tenure and eligibility.
