What a personal loan actually is

A personal loan is a fixed sum, lent against nothing but your credit profile and your ability to repay. There is no property to mortgage, no gold to deposit, no guarantor to arrange. The lender reads three things — your income, your existing obligations, and your repayment history — and decides both whether to lend and at what price.

Because nothing secures it, the rate sits well above a home loan or a loan against property. Today the market runs from roughly 10.25% to 24% per annum, and where you land inside that band is almost entirely a function of your CIBIL score and your employer category. A 780 score with a salary account at a large private bank gets a very different number from a 690 score at a small firm — on exactly the same income.

The tenure runs from twelve months to seven years. Longer tenure means a smaller EMI and a much larger total interest bill, so the right answer is the shortest tenure whose EMI you can comfortably carry, not the longest one you qualify for.

A personal loan is expensive money used well, or very expensive money used badly. The product doesn't decide which — the purpose does.

What people use it for

  • Wedding expenses, where timing is fixed and costs land together
  • Medical bills not covered, or not fully covered, by insurance
  • Consolidating several credit-card balances into one lower-rate EMI
  • Home renovation or interiors that no home loan will fund
  • A child's admission fee or semester payment with a hard deadline
  • Travel, relocation or a deposit that has to be paid before a salary cycle

Types of Personal Loans we arrange

Not every unsecured loan is the same product. The right variant depends on your profile, and picking correctly can move your rate by several percentage points.

Salaried personal loan

Priced off your salary credit, employer category and CIBIL. The most competitive segment, and the quickest to disburse.

Self-employed personal loan

Assessed on ITR, business vintage and banking turnover. Needs more paperwork but opens larger ticket sizes.

Balance transfer & top-up

Move an existing high-rate personal loan to a cheaper lender, and draw additional funds on the same file.

Professional loan

Dedicated programmes for doctors, chartered accountants, architects and other qualified professionals, often at finer rates.

Debt consolidation

One loan replacing several card balances and small EMIs — lower blended rate, one due date, easier to track.

Pre-approved offers

If you already bank with a lender, there may be a pre-approved line sitting unused. We check this first, before applying anywhere.

Who qualifies

Lenders set their own cut-offs, but these are the broad bars across the network. Falling short on one criterion does not disqualify you — it usually just moves you to a different lender.

Salaried applicants

  • Age 21 to 60 years at loan maturity
  • Net monthly income from ₹15,000 (₹25,000+ in metros)
  • Minimum 6 months in the current job; 1–2 years total work experience
  • Salary credited to a bank account, not cash
  • CIBIL 700+ for bank pricing; 650+ for NBFC programmes

Self-employed applicants

  • Age 23 to 65 years at loan maturity
  • Business continuity of 2 to 3 years
  • ITR-filed income above the lender's threshold
  • Consistent current-account turnover
  • No recent cheque bounces or EMI defaults

Documents you'll need

Send clear soft copies on WhatsApp and we will pre-check the whole set before anything goes to a lender. A file that is complete on day one is the single biggest reason a sanction comes back fast.

Salaried applicants

  • PAN card and Aadhaar card
  • Last 3 months' salary slips
  • Last 6 months' salary-account bank statement
  • Form 16 or latest ITR
  • Employee ID / appointment or confirmation letter
  • 2 passport-size photographs

Self-employed / business

  • PAN card and Aadhaar card
  • Last 2 years' ITR with computation of income
  • Audited P&L and balance sheet (where applicable)
  • Last 12 months' current-account statement
  • GST registration and last 6–12 months' GST returns
  • Business proof — Udyam, Shop Act, partnership deed or MOA/AOA

Also keep handy

  • Existing loan sanction letters and repayment schedules
  • Latest credit-card statements
  • Rent agreement or utility bill as address proof
  • Cancelled cheque of the account for EMI mandate

How to get a better deal

Fix the score before the application, not after

A jump from 690 to 740 can cut your rate by two to three percentage points. Clear card dues, bring usage below 30% of the limit, and wait a cycle. Six weeks of patience is often worth more than any negotiation.

Apply once, not everywhere

Every application is a hard enquiry on your report. Six applications in a month reads as desperation to an underwriter, and the rejections themselves become a reason for the next rejection. One matched application beats six hopeful ones.

Shorten the tenure if the EMI allows

A ₹5 lakh loan at 13% costs about ₹1.7 lakh in interest over five years and about ₹92,000 over three. Same loan, same rate — the tenure is doing all the damage.

Negotiate the processing fee, not just the rate

Lenders have far more room on the fee than on the rate. On a ₹20 lakh sanction, 1% versus 2% is ₹20,000 plus GST back in your pocket, available simply for asking.

If you already pay a car or home loan EMI, that capacity shrinks accordingly. The honest answer needs your actual obligations, which takes us about five minutes to work through.
