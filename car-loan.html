What the showroom will not explain carefully

Car finance is straightforward: the lender holds a hypothecation on the vehicle until the loan closes, and that security keeps the rate low. New car loans currently start around 7.40% per annum and run to about 14% depending on profile and lender. Used car loans begin higher, around 10.45%, because a second-hand vehicle depreciates faster and is harder to value.

The first thing to get right is what is being financed. Ex-showroom price excludes road tax, registration and insurance. On-road price includes them and is usually 8% to 12% higher. Lenders advertising "100% funding" generally mean 100% of ex-showroom, which still leaves you paying the difference in cash. A few lenders fund the full on-road price for strong profiles, and that is worth asking for by name.

The second is where you arrange the loan. The dealership's finance desk is convenient and is paid a commission on what it places — convenience that frequently costs you half a percent or more, plus a bundled insurance or extended-warranty product you did not price. Walking in with a sanction already in hand changes that conversation, and usually the price of the car along with it.

Arrange the finance before you negotiate the car. A buyer who does not need the showroom's loan negotiates from a completely different position.

What people use it for

  • Buying a new car from a showroom

Types of Car Loans we arrange

Each of these is underwritten differently, and the rate gap between them is wide. Make sure you are applying for the one you actually need.

New car loan

The sharpest pricing in vehicle finance. Up to 100% of ex-showroom, tenure to seven years, quick approval at approved dealerships.

Used car loan

Funds up to 85% of the lender's assessed valuation — not the asking price. Rate is two to three points above a new car loan.

Green / EV car loan

Several banks price electric and hybrid vehicles below their standard car rate, with longer tenure. Ask for it explicitly; it is rarely offered.

Two-wheeler loan

Small ticket, quick approval, tenure of one to four years. Priced higher in percentage terms but low in absolute cost.

Commercial vehicle loan

For taxis, pickups and light trucks used in business. Assessed on route viability and earning potential alongside your income.

Loan against car

Your existing, fully-paid car becomes security for cash. Cheaper than a personal loan when the car is recent and well-maintained.

Who qualifies

Car loan eligibility is simpler than most products, because the asset itself carries much of the risk. The vehicle still has to qualify, though — particularly a used one.

Applicant criteria

  • Age 21 to 65 years at loan maturity
  • Salaried: net income from ₹20,000 per month, 1 year in the job
  • Self-employed: 2 years of business with filed ITR
  • CIBIL 700+ for the best rate; options exist from 650
  • Valid driving licence
  • Stable residence — 1 year at the current address

Vehicle criteria

  • New: invoice from an authorised dealer
  • Used: age typically under 8 to 10 years at loan maturity
  • Clear RC with no existing hypothecation
  • Valid insurance and pollution certificate
  • Satisfactory valuation and physical inspection, for used vehicles
  • Vehicle registered in the applicant's name after disbursal

Documents you'll need

Send clear soft copies on WhatsApp and we will pre-check the whole set before anything goes to a lender. A file that is complete on day one is the single biggest reason a sanction comes back fast.

Salaried applicants

  • PAN card and Aadhaar card
  • Last 3 months' salary slips
  • Last 6 months' salary-account bank statement
  • Form 16 or latest ITR
  • Employee ID / appointment or confirmation letter
  • 2 passport-size photographs

Self-employed / business

  • PAN card and Aadhaar card
  • Last 2 years' ITR with computation of income
  • Audited P&L and balance sheet (where applicable)
  • Last 12 months' current-account statement
  • GST registration and last 6–12 months' GST returns
  • Business proof — Udyam, Shop Act, partnership deed or MOA/AOA

Vehicle documents

  • Proforma invoice or quotation from the dealer
  • For used cars: RC, insurance, PUC and the seller's KYC
  • Valuation report for a used vehicle
  • Copy of the driving licence
  • NOC from the existing financier, if the car was financed before

How to get a better deal

Get sanctioned before you walk into the showroom

A buyer holding a sanction letter negotiates on the car, not on the EMI. Dealership finance desks earn on placement, and that commission is paid out of your rate.

Compare on-road, not ex-showroom

A quoted 100% funding usually means 100% of ex-showroom. On a ₹10 lakh car, the on-road figure can be ₹11.2 lakh — so you are still finding ₹1.2 lakh in cash. Ask what the actual disbursal amount will be.

Ask your existing bank first

Many banks give 25 to 50 basis points of concession to customers who already hold a salary account or a home loan with them. It takes one phone call and is frequently the best rate on the table.

Unbundle the insurance

Dealer-arranged motor insurance is convenient and generally expensive. Compare two outside quotes for the same IDV and cover. The saving often exceeds the entire processing fee.
